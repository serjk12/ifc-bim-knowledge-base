## In plain words

A researcher in this knowledge base is someone who uses IFC files **as evidence or raw material**, not to deliver a building. Typical people: a PhD or master's student writing a thesis; a university lecturer who teaches openBIM; a member of a buildingSMART working group or a national research institute; a tool developer at a research lab who builds a converter, a checker or an AI demo; a sustainability consultant who needs material quantities for a life-cycle assessment (LCA).

What makes them different from the developer persona is the **question behind the question**. A developer asks "why does my code crash?". A researcher asks "is this a property of IFC, of the exporter, or of my method, and can I cite it?". They want reproducible numbers, the exact schema name, a licence for the dataset, and an honest statement of what the tool could not do. They are also the most **sceptical about AI**: the data contains a researcher who fine-tuned a small model on 42,680 IFC question-answer pairs and then listed its remaining errors (`ja-aimcp-r034`), and a Chinese article asking whether AI can really read drawings and build BIM models (`zh-zhihu-r042`).

Three behaviours are characteristic and worth testing:

1. **Many of them write in a formal, structured register**: numbered options (A to G), numbered questions, test matrices across operating systems, version numbers in the first line (`en-bsforum-r019`, `en-osarch-r003`, `en-osarch-r008`).
2. **They often do not attach the file or the code.** A short, non-native-English bug report that says "I'm researching IFC to CityGML, shapes are broken, can you look at my code?" with nothing attached is real (`en-gh-gis-r001`); the maintainer's only possible answer was "send code, commands and a test model".
3. **They distrust a convenient answer.** Typical reaction to "it works": "show me how you computed it", "which version?", "where does the spec say that?".

Evidence caveat: most tagged records are English forum and GitHub items, plus academic papers and theses in Chinese and Korean that were found only as titles or search snippets (the `zh-lightweight-review` and `ko-*` rows are from abstract or listing level, not full text). Real Russian, Spanish and German researcher questions are rare, so prompts in those languages below are mostly synthetic.

## Goals

- **Get numbers they can defend.** A volume, an element count, a count of lost elements, with the method named (Qto value or geometry? which unit? which version of the tool?).
- **Move the model into their own formalism**: a graph, a triple store, a table, an analysis model, a city model. Keep GlobalIds so results can be traced back.
- **Understand what is standard and what is exporter behaviour.** Is the roof-as-thin-slabs problem IFC, Revit, or the viewer? (`en-gh-proxy-r032`)
- **Show that the data is sufficient for a method**: LCA needs per-layer quantities (`en-qto-r026`), deconstruction analysis needs element connections (`en-mep-r002`), energy simulation needs the right space boundaries (`en-spaces-r027`).
- **Teach without vendor lock-in.** A course that stays software-neutral, with materials in the local language (`p-student-r003`).
- **Publish and cite.** Open datasets with a clear licence (`nl-dutch-r016`), a reproducible project template (`en-osarch-r003`), a repository with a BibTeX entry (`en-aimcp-r039`).
- **Test whether AI helps.** Find the limits of a language model on IFC questions, not just the successes (`ja-aimcp-r034`).

## Vocabulary they use (and what they do not know)

**Words they use, often correctly and in the schema's own spelling.**

| Area | Terms |
|---|---|
| Data and semantics | ifcOWL, RDF, Turtle, SPARQL, ontology, SWRL, graph, triple, ifcSQL, referential integrity, SELECT type, EXPRESS |
| Schema identity | `IFC2x3`, `IFC4`, `IFC4X3_ADD2` (the EXPRESS schema identifier), "IFC 4.3" (the name in prose), MVD, IDM, IDS, LOIN, DTV (Design Transfer View), Reference View, CSG |
| Geometry and place | `IfcMapConversion`, `IfcProjectedCRS`, EPSG, `IfcIShapeProfileDef`, `FlangeSlope`, tessellation, precision, tetrahedra, boundary representation |
| Relations | `IfcRelSpaceBoundary` (1st and 2nd level), `IfcRelConnectsElements`, `IfcRelConnectsPathElements`, `IfcRelAssociatesMaterial`, `IsDefinedBy` |
| Materials and sustainability | LCA, EPD, GWP, modules A1 to C4, `IfcPropertyTableValue`, `IfcPropertyListValue`, `IfcMaterialLayerSet`, `IfcMaterialConstituentSet` |
| City and energy | CityGML, LoD, BEM (building energy model), EnergyPlus, gbXML |
| AI and evaluation | MCP, FastMCP, fine-tuning, LoRA, ROUGE, BLEU, benchmark, hallucination, tokens |
| Research hygiene | reproducible, test matrix, minimal example, licence, CC BY 4.0, BibTeX, "cite us" |

**What they usually do not know.** These are the gaps to expect in role-play, based on how the questions are phrased.

- **Why a practitioner exports the way they do.** A researcher asks whether Revit "exports connections"; the answer (only `IfcRelConnectsPathElements`) comes from export-tool behaviour, not from the standard (`en-mep-r002`).
- **Exporter switches and shared parameters**, such as `IfcExportAs` or export-setup details: one asker found the answer was geometry splitting, not classification (`en-gh-proxy-r032`).
- **Practical limits of viewers.** They assume that a property placed in the file is shown somewhere; thermal material properties in IFC2x3 are attributes and do not appear as property sets in the viewer (`en-gh-ifcopenshell-r012`).
- **Real project conditions**: what a contractor or designer will actually fill in; why a "perfect" schema option (table value) is unusable when authoring tools only write flat single values (`en-bsforum-r019`).
- **Other countries' rules.** Korean, Chinese, Taiwanese, Russian and Latin American items are written from inside each country's framework and rarely compare to others.
- **Spelling of tool names**: an asker wrote "OpenCeepy" for OpenSeesPy (`en-struct-r019`); the assistant should quietly resolve it.

## How to role-play this persona in tests

**Voice.** Careful, formal, often structured. The opener frequently gives the research context ("I'm researching IFC to CityGML" in `en-gh-gis-r001`; a stated deconstruction-analysis purpose, polite greeting and thanks in `en-mep-r002`); trainers and teachers write long, polite posts with numbered questions (`en-osarch-r003`). Test numbers appear early (file sizes, versions, 1,025 elements, 2.8 vs 2.75). Quote spec entity names exactly; quote tool errors verbatim even in a different language (a German XML error inside an English report, `de-gh-gis-r005`). Informal exceptions exist: a short, no-attachment bug report from a non-native speaker (`en-gh-gis-r001`) and a Chinese idiom opening a sceptical essay (`zh-zhihu-r042`). English example: *"for an LCA I need volume per material layer of every external wall, not just thickness"* (modelled on `en-qto-r026`); Russian: *"можно ли требование из СП автоматически превратить в IDS?"*; Chinese: *"原生模型是1200个构件，导成IFC之后有多少个？"*; Japanese: *"FlangeSlopeに値が入っている梁を探して"*.

**Typical context.** A thesis, paper, course or research prototype; a named tool and version (IfcOpenShell 0.8, Bonsai 0.9, Revit exporter); a public dataset (Schependomlaan, FZK, BIM4LCA samples; see [sample IFC files](../../04_open_source/sample_ifc_files.md)); a target representation (RDF, graph, CityGML, STEP, OpenSees, spreadsheet). The file is often not attached, or confidential, or very large.

**Knowledge level.**
- Strong: ontologies and data models, literature, evaluation methods, statistics, programming (often Python), the IFC schema as a document.
- Medium: viewers and the common authoring tools; the difference between MVD, IDM and IDS (asked about repeatedly).
- Weak: what real offices do, exporter switches, shared parameters, what practitioners will actually fill in, rules of other countries.
- Believes: that a property seen in the spec is also in the file; that an odd result is either a tool bug or an IFC problem and wants to know which.

**What to test with this persona.**
- Numbers computed from the real file with the method named (counts, volumes, fill rates) and a script the user can repeat (`ra-s006`, `ra-s025`).
- Honesty about missing data: per-layer quantities, connections, weight, density, boundaries (`ra-s001`, `ra-s003`, `ra-s010`).
- Spec ambiguity: say "the specification does not define this" instead of inventing a rule (`ra-s035`, `ra-s008`).
- Conversion caveats: ifcOWL lists, graph direction, CityGML LoD, units on STEP export (`en-data-s007`, `ra-s004`, `ra-s012`, `ra-s013`).
- Tool limits: precision far from the origin, diff by id vs value, scale flags (`ra-s011`, `ra-s007`).
- Plain teaching explanations built from the real file (`ra-s015`, `ra-s030`, `ra-s045`).
- Country regulation: the assistant must ask for the rule text or say which checks are generic (`ra-s031`, `ra-s032`, `ra-s040`, `ra-s038`).
- AI self-assessment: what can be edited, what is only read, no claim of an "official validation" (`en-osarch-s017`, `ra-s039`, `ra-s019`).

**Typical follow-ups.**
1. "Which version of the tool and the schema did you use?" / "какая версия библиотеки?"
2. "Is that in the standard or is it how Revit/Archicad exports it?"
3. "Show me the script so I can put it in the appendix."
4. "How many elements are missing the value, not just how many have it?"
5. "Can you do the same for the other file / the previous week's model and compare?"
6. "Give me the source for that statement." (expects a reference to the schema documentation or a record, not a confident guess)
7. "Now save the result as CSV / RDF / graph and tell me what was lost."

**Quick judge checklist.**
- Did the assistant inspect the file (header, schema, counts) rather than recite documentation?
- Were results reproducible: method, units, tool and schema version stated?
- Did it separate "not in the IFC standard", "not exported by this tool" and "not in this file"?
- Did it say "the spec is silent" or "this data is absent" when that is the truth, and avoid inventing a rule, a clause number or a licence conclusion?
- For conversions: did it name what was lost (lists, units, representation detail)?
- For regulation questions: did it ask for the rule text instead of pretending to know it?
- Did it avoid presenting its own work as an official validation or certification?
