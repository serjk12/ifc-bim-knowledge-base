## Top themes (ranked by frequency)
- Revit/Archicad IFC export defects: wrong class (proxy), dislocated geometry, missing colors/materials, missing Psets or mapped shared parameters
- Clash detection: running it, grouping hundreds of clashes into actionable issues, tool errors, BCF output
- Version comparison when GlobalIds are regenerated by the exporter (GUID churn)
- Model checking against requirements (IDS, missing properties, classification, naming)
- Naming of elements (Name vs Reference vs Description, deprecated attributes, vendor limits)
- Multi-file/federated project structure (root IFC referencing discipline files)
- Coordinates, origins, storeys and levels consistency across disciplines
- MEP specifics: systems (IfcSystem), duct/pipe Pset mapping, colors
- Opening/void handling and viewer warnings when importing Revit IFC
- Large-file performance and viewers for coordination models
- Vendor-tool gripes (Navisworks) being redirected off openBIM forums
- Learning paths for Revit users moving to open-source tools
- Handover data (COBie) and exporting model data to databases

## What people want (the goals behind the questions)
- A reliable weekly export that arrives in the federated model with correct classes, properties, storeys and coordinates
- Fast answers to "what is missing or wrong" in a model (counts, lists by storey, GlobalIds) before the coordination meeting
- Clash lists that are grouped by system/storey/cause instead of thousands of raw hits, with issues passed on as BCF
- Diffs between today's and last week's model that survive regenerated GUIDs
- To know whether a defect is the exporter's fault, the settings, or the viewer, and who to report it to
- Free or open tools that replace Navisworks/Solibri-style checks, plus training for them

## How people write here
- Register: informal, short (2 to 5 sentences), many non-native English speakers; beginners open with "Hi all", "Hello community", "G'day all" and close with thanks in advance.
- Little context: file/software version often omitted; attachments requested by responders ("attach the IFC").
- Jargon: proxy (IfcBuildingElementProxy), GUID churn, voids/openings, "mapping table", "federate", BCF, Pset, "parts", Void Limit.
- Titles often just state symptom: "Revit to IFC, Wall is dislocated".
- Fragments: "i struggle since one year"; "Can anyone give pointer where it went wrong?"; "or I missed something"; "shed some light"; "Bug or intended?".
- Developers write more precisely and disclose products; meta pain-point surveys get no answers.
- Japanese blog posts use a failure-story title style ("could not do it") and Revit API vocabulary.
- Tone: mostly neutral/polite, occasional frustration about Revit colors/materials and vendor limits.
