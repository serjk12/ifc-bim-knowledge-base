# Africa: South Africa, Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana BIM communities: IFC questions

Cell: en-africa · Languages: English · Collected: 2026-11-06 · Records: 24 real / 16 synthetic · Communities covered: OSArch forum (posts by users in South Africa, Nigeria, Ghana), cidb South Africa (official). Kenya: nothing found.

Important limitation: the WebSearch budget was exhausted before any search ran, so everything comes from direct WebFetch of OSArch pages (via its site search) and the cidb site. The picture is dominated by open-source/Bonsai (BlenderBIM) users, not by Revit or commercial-BIM practitioners.

## Top themes (ranked by frequency)
- Quantities, BoQ, rate build-up and cost estimating from IFC (QS voice, kg/m2 and kg/m3 rebar conventions)
- 4D/5D scheduling and costing, CSV import errors
- Georeferencing, CRS/EPSG, UTM units, federating models
- Adoption barriers: convincing colleagues, documentation too crude, management hesitant about openBIM
- Council/permit drawing packs: schedules, door swing, sewer connections, local window types
- Local code checks (SANS 10400 XA glazing/ventilation ratios) vs imported standards (TM59, IBC)
- Classification systems (custom, Uniclass, OmniClass) and psets
- MEP terminology (fittings, bends, tees) and IFC 4.3 naming
- Stair/nosing and other detailed element parameters
- Open-source tool choice vs Revit; remote paid work with Bonsai
- National BIM policy (cidb strategy) with no IFC/openBIM language
- AI/LLM tools generating IFC (text-to-IFC) and AI in open-source projects

## What people want (the goals behind the questions)
- Produce a bill of quantities and cost estimate straight from the model with local measurement and rate conventions.
- Get 4D/5D planning without expensive commercial tools (Synchro, Primavera integration).
- Deliver council submission drawings and schedules quickly from an IFC-based tool.
- Align and federate models by real-world coordinates without manual origin fiddling.
- Use free/open tools because of licence cost and local adoption realities.
- Understand how national BIM strategy translates into concrete file requirements.

## How people write here
- Register: conversational professional English, first-person intros ("QS with 15 years in Nigeria"), long wish-lists from architects, short terse UI complaints from technical users.
- Typical length: one to three short paragraphs; lists of missing features are long.
- Jargon: BoQ/BOQ, "rate build-up" (Ghana, = unit price build-up), preliminaries, schedule of rates, council submission, nosing, SANS 10400 XA, UTM zone 30N, Qto, "game changer".
- Misspellings/variants: "Bender" for Blender, mixed BlenderBIM/Bonsai naming, "ifc" lowercase, "thru".
- Openers/closers: self-introduction then goal; thanks; frequently praise the developers' fast response.
- Context given: software + version often named, country mentioned as a justification for local convention ("in Ghana mesh is quoted kg/m2"); files rarely attached.
- Tone: curious and polite, with frustration over adoption and tool gaps.
- Fragments: "not suitable for Quantity Surveyors"; "hardest part is getting colleagues to adopt"; "documentation is still crude"; "Key Error: Activity/Resource Name".
